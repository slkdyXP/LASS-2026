# Figure captions

## Main figure

**Cognitive overgeneralization propagates into persistent action error and domain-specific cost.** **a**, Late cognitive overgeneralization at round 12 across three domains and three intervention conditions. **b**, Absolute deviation of the final three actions from the pre-intervention baseline. Points show condition means and error bars show non-parametric bootstrap 95% confidence intervals across domain × repeat trajectories. **c**, Post-recovery harm relative to the matched no-shock trajectory, normalized by each domain's action/outcome range; positive values indicate additional harm. **d**, Association between matched shock-induced cognitive change and persistent action change (n=90 matched cells; Spearman ρ=0.341, descriptive p=0.000992). Rule-based partners isolate the causal pathway from focal-agent cognition to downstream outcomes. Five independent API trajectories were run per method × domain × condition cell.

## Trajectory figure

**Post-event action recovery differs across memory methods.** Mean focal-agent action over 12 rounds for named-other and world shocks in the fishery (**a,b**), water auction (**c,d**), and public-goods game (**e,f**). Shading denotes mean ± 1.96 s.e.m. across five API trajectories. The dashed line and grey band mark the intervention settlement at round 4. Full History and scope-aware memory generally return to the verified equilibrium, whereas Reflection exhibits prolonged under-harvesting, bid escalation, and transient contribution changes.

## Identity-confusion figure

**Reflection converts the focal agent's own settlement identity into a competitor.** **a**, Percentage of auction trajectories containing a post-event action reason/message that explicitly describes Erin—the focal agent's assigned identity—as a third-person bidder to beat, tie, or anticipate. Error bars are Wilson 95% intervals; fractions show counts out of 15 auction trajectories. **b**, Unnecessary post-recovery winning-bid expenditure relative to the sufficient bid of 50. Dots are trajectories; black bars show means and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Reflection produced explicit self-as-competitor confusion in 8/15 trajectories, compared with 0/15 for both controls (Reflection versus Full History, Fisher exact p=0.00220).
